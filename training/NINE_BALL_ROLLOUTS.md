# Chernika — break learning and complete-rack nine-ball

## Scope

This phase moves evaluation from one shot per randomized position to a complete seeded nine-ball rack. It remains an offline simulator experiment. No Android APK, live Plato gestures, or screen-capture code is changed.

The rack is the calibrated 1-2-3-2-1 diamond. Ball 1 is the front apex, ball 9 is the center ball, and balls 2–8 are shuffled. The existing 52-value observation and shared nine-ball rule assessor are retained.

## Break learning

The break path uses a separate provisional cue-speed multiplier (4.0× the ordinary-shot calibration) and searches direct-apex plus both second-row aim lines. The multiplier is based on the upper end of earlier Plato trajectory estimates and is deliberately isolated from normal shots. It is not considered final calibration until the original break clip's cue start, aim line and settled positions are rechecked.

A separate 52-input regression policy learns the two normalized break controls (angle offset and power). It is deliberately separate from the six-pocket post-break policy so that break actions do not corrupt pocket-classification labels.

Generate teacher demonstrations, train and evaluate:

    python -m chernika_rl.generate_nine_ball_break_data --racks 400 --seed 91000
    python -m chernika_rl.train_nine_ball_break --data artifacts/nine_ball_break_dataset.npz --epochs 100
    python -m chernika_rl.evaluate_nine_ball_break --model artifacts/nine_ball_break_policy.pt --episodes 120 --seed 92000

The held-out break evaluation compares the physics teacher with the raw neural break action on identical seeded racks. It reports legal-break rate, foul/scratch/off-table rates, balls pocketed, nine-on-break wins and the number of distinct object balls reaching a cushion. The raw neural action is not assumed to be safe or better.

## Multi-shot planning

The new two-ply planner evaluates candidate first shots and then simulates the best available legal continuation from each of the strongest first-shot candidates. It uses the same calibrated physics and nine-ball rule assessor. The planner returns only the first action; the continuation is a value estimate, not a pre-committed sequence.

The neural post-break policy ranks up to three pocket candidates. The planner then verifies those candidates with full-table multi-ball physics. If the restricted set produces no legal plan, it falls back to the full six-pocket physics search. The break remains teacher-controlled in this phase until break-policy validation supports a safer hybrid.

## Break-derived imitation data

Generate states by running seeded racks: the physics teacher breaks, then a two-ply teacher plans subsequent legal shots. The resulting dataset contains post-break observations, action labels, pocket labels, teacher scores, success flags and first-contact flags. This is a more relevant training distribution than randomly scattered stationary balls.

    python -m chernika_rl.generate_nine_ball_rollout_data --racks 60 --seed 93000 --max-shots 24 --depth 2
    python -m chernika_rl.train_imitation --data artifacts/nine_ball_rollout_dataset.npz --epochs 80 --min-demonstrations 50

## Complete-rack evaluation

    python -m chernika_rl.evaluate_nine_ball_rollouts --model artifacts/nine_ball_rollout_policy.pt --episodes 20 --seed 94000 --depth 2 --top-k 3

Three controllers are evaluated on the same rack seeds as player 0, each against the same greedy physics-teacher opponent:

- Greedy physics teacher: one-shot scoring with full six-pocket search.
- Two-ply physics teacher: short-horizon continuation scoring.
- Neural top-3 + two-ply physics: learned pocket ranking followed by simulator verification.

The report includes rack win rate, legal-break rate, break spread, foul rate, no-plan rate, mean shots, mean balls pocketed, shots to win and a per-shot trajectory. The trajectory records actions, cue positions, first contacts, pocketed numbers, remaining balls, foul reasons and planner scores to support later replay comparison.

## Interpretation and limitations

A rack win means the 9 is legally pocketed by the single practice agent. It is not a match win against an opponent. A foul ends the current practice rollout; opponent turns, ball-in-hand, 9-ball respotting after fouls and tactical safety exchanges are not yet simulated.

The lookahead is depth two, not a complete game-tree search. The planner enumerates direct pots, one-cushion object-ball banks, one-cushion cue-ball kicks and a legal safety fallback. Multi-cushion routes and deliberate combination-shot families are not yet explicit. Spin, throw, masse, detailed pocket-jaw behavior and cloth anisotropy remain unmodelled.

The break and post-break models are evaluated on synthetic racks generated by the current Python physics mirror. These results do not establish accuracy on live Plato. Before Android integration, the next gates are:
1. Validate Python/Java golden-shot agreement.
2. Compare predicted trajectories with real Plato replays, including breaks.
3. Add ball-in-hand, 9-ball respotting, banks, combinations and safety play.
4. Require held-out complete-rack performance and replay agreement before any live firing.


## Expanded shot families and turn-aware rack evaluation

The post-break planner now tries shot families in a conservative order:

1. Direct object-ball pots.
2. One-cushion object-ball banks, using reflected-pocket geometry.
3. One-cushion cue-ball kicks, using reflected-cue geometry.
4. Legal safety shots when no potting route survives full physics and rule validation.

Every candidate is simulated against all remaining balls. A bank or kick is accepted only when the shared nine-ball assessor confirms first contact on the lowest-numbered ball and a legal pot. Safety candidates must make the same legal first contact and satisfy the post-contact rail requirement; the safety score rewards cue-to-target separation and hiding the lowest ball behind a blocker.

Complete-rack evaluation now alternates turns: a legal pot retains the shooter, while a legal no-pot shot passes to the opponent. Player 0 uses the evaluated controller and player 1 uses the greedy physics teacher. This makes safety handoffs meaningful rather than allowing one controller to keep shooting after a legal safety.

The imitation pocket classifier is trained only on potting demonstrations with valid six-pocket labels. Safety actions are executed in teacher self-play rollouts but excluded from that classifier's labels; the physics planner remains responsible for the safety fallback.

Remaining limitations: only one-cushion banks and kicks are enumerated; multi-rail routes, deliberate combinations, spin/throw, push-outs, stalemate re-racks and live Plato replay validation are not yet implemented.


## Match-level foul recovery and ball-in-hand

The complete-rack evaluator now uses a match-mode shot environment. After any foul, the incoming player receives ball-in-hand anywhere on the cloth. The cue ball is removed from its old position, and a deterministic placement search scores a small set of legal cloth-grid and direct-pot ghost-ball positions with the incoming player's controller before selecting the best placement.

If the 9-ball was pocketed or driven off the table on a foul, it is respotted on the original #1 apex position. If that spot is occupied, the search moves up-screen toward the foot rail first, then down-screen toward the head rail; other object balls are not respotted. Three consecutive fouls by one player lose the rack. These transitions follow the WPA 9-ball foul, spotting and three-foul provisions.

The default Gym training environment still terminates on fouls. Only the complete-match evaluator opts into recoverable-foul mode, so existing single-shot training semantics remain unchanged.

Still outstanding: push-out decisions, the optional three-ball break rule, stalemate re-racks, full opponent strength diversity, multi-rail bank/kick search, spin/throw and live Plato replay validation.


### One-cushion kick safety fallback

When direct safety contacts are blocked, the teacher now unfolds the cue-ball path across each of the four cushions and tests a one-rail kick into the lowest-numbered ball. Each candidate must pass the same physics and nine-ball legality checks as other shots. Kick safeties retain a distinct internal shot type but are excluded from all six-pocket imitation labels.


## Plato-specific rule corrections

The match engine follows the project's observed Plato 9-ball rulebook rather than generic WPA defaults:

- A break may contact any object ball first; missing every object ball is an illegal break.
- A no-pot break requires at least three distinct object balls to make at least four total object-ball cushion contacts. Cue-ball rail contacts do not count toward this break requirement.
- A pocketed object ball satisfies the break pot-or-cushion requirement, but a scratch or other foul still applies.
- An illegal no-pot break is reracked and the opponent breaks from behind the head string. Break scratches use the documented behind-head-string branches; pocketed non-9 balls stay down.
- Ordinary ball-in-hand is anywhere on the cloth, and touching another ball is permitted as long as balls do not overlap.
- A 9-ball pocketed on a foul is respotted at the original #1 apex coordinate (306.5, 354.5), not the original #9 centre. If blocked, the simulator chooses the nearest non-overlapping open position around that apex.
- Three ordinary shot fouls do not automatically lose the rack.

The head-string coordinate is currently a provisional one-quarter-table estimate because the exact white-line pixel position has not yet been measured from the Plato screenshot. It must be calibrated before live break-foul placement is considered pixel-exact.
